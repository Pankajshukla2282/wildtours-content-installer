## Post 3 — Safari Rules & Packing (`/blog/panna-safari-rules-packing-list/`)

**SEO Title:** Panna Safari Rules + Packing Checklist | Panna Wild Tour
**Meta Description:** Forest rules for Panna safaris — IDs, silence, staying inside the gypsy, cameras, what to wear and pack. The checklist our naturalists give every guest.

```html
<!-- wp:heading {"level":1} -->
<h1>Panna Safari Rules &amp; Packing Checklist</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>A few rules keep everyone safe and the wildlife wild. Follow these and your guide will quietly thank you — and your photographs will be better for it.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2>Forest rules we never break</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --><li>Carry one valid government photo ID per person (exact match to permit).</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li>Stay inside the gypsy — no alighting outside designated stops.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li>Silence is your best tracking tool; no loud calls, no phones on speakers.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li>No feeding, no litter, no plastic inside the reserve.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li>Cameras may need a permit/fee per forest rules — we confirm before the drive.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li>Respect the guide's instructions — they answer to the forest department first.</li><!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2>Packing checklist</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>Wear:</strong> earthy colours (khaki, olive, brown) — quiet fabrics, full-sleeve for sun and insects.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>Layer:</strong> early-morning drives are cold in winter (5–10°C); mornings start warm in summer.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>Carry:</strong> water, hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, small binoculars.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>Shoot:</strong> 70–200mm (or 100–400mm) lens; phone holders for the gypsy bar; spare batteries.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>Skip:</strong> bright colours, loud jewellery, strong perfume, tripods inside the reserve.</li><!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2>Morning slot survival</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Reporting is 30–45 minutes before gate opening — summer 05:30, winter 06:30. Keep breakfast light and eat after the drive if you can; your lodge will pack something.</p>
```

## Post 4 — Vultures of Panna (`/blog/vultures-of-panna-conservation-story/`)

**SEO Title:** Vultures of Panna Tiger Reserve — Where & How to See Them | Panna Wild Tour
**Meta Description:** Panna's cliffs host India's rare vultures. Learn where the gharial-adjacent Ken river cliffs hold white-rumped and Indian vultures, and how to spot them on safari or by boat.

```html
<!-- wp:heading {"level":1} -->
<h1>Vultures of Panna</h1>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Panna quietly shelters some of India's rarest birds. On the tall sandstone cliffs above the Ken river, and in the dry forest canopy, you can find white-rumped, Indian, Egyptian and red-headed vultures — species that vanished from most of the country's skies over the past two decades.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2>Why Panna matters for vultures</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Safe nesting ledges, low disturbance and a functioning ecosystem let these colonial nesters hold on here. The reserve is part of the Ken River Gharial Sanctuary landscape — a rare overlap where top scavengers and top reptiles share the same river.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2>How and where to see them</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>By boat:</strong> Ken river sections with cliff walls — watch for nests on ledges and birds kettling on thermals.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>On safari:</strong> scan dead snags and open ridges; vultures often signal a recent kill nearby — a useful wildlife cue.</li><!-- /wp:list-item -->
<!-- wp:list-item --><li><strong>Timing:</strong> late mornings, when thermals build, are prime.</li><!-- /wp:list-item --></ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":2} -->
<h2>A birder's bonus</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Pair vulture-watching with kingfishers, terns and crested serpent eagles on the river, and the reserve's broader dry-forest birds — a genuinely special morning for any birdwatcher.</p>
```
